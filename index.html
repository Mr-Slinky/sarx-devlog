Sarx

Sarx is a god game for Windows, built by hand in Rust. It's the successor to Black and White 2 that I wish someone else had made!

About this log

I write an entry on each day that I work on Sarx. Some days it's a win, like the hand finally standing above the ground. Other days it's me admitting that I have no idea what skinning is yet!

Engines such as Unity and Bevy already provide most of what Sarx needs: drawing the world, running the game loop and loading models. I build those parts myself anyway, and I know it's the long way round! The first reason is that I can't change what a layer does until I understand it. The second is that I want Sarx to try new things in its genre, like villagers that shatter instead of bleed.

For now, the code is public on GitHub. Once a small demo is out, the code will move to a private repository. From then on, this log will be the public record of how the game is made.

The game

I played Black and White 2 as a kid, and fell in love with it all over again in my mid-20s. When I went looking for a modern successor, the best I found was Godus, and it lacked SO MUCH character. There was no hand or creature, and the map looked like a world made of pancakes. So I'm making my own.

In Sarx, you play a god, and you reach into the world through a large, disembodied hand. A creature lives among your followers and learns from how you treat it. Up to three rival factions share the map, and you win by defeating each one, either by siege or by building a city so impressive that its people move to yours.

Here's my own twist: the villagers are made of materials rather than flesh, and the material decides how a villager breaks.

  • A villager made of clay splatters.
  • A villager made of glass shatters.
  • A villager made of wood splinters.

Sarx is Greek for "flesh". Ironic...

How the code is split

I've split the game into five Rust crates (for now), and named each one after a god whose domain matches its job. The entries call the crates by these names, so here's who's who.

Atum
The first of the Egyptian gods, who created himself
Opens the window and starts the rest of the game.
Zurvan
The Persian god of infinite time
Moves the world forward, one fixed step after another.
Enki
The Mesopotamian god of wisdom and crafts
Stores every entity in the world, and the components that define it.
Amaterasu
The Japanese goddess of the sun
Draws the world on the screen, through the GPU.
Hermes
The Greek messenger of the gods
Reads 3D models, such as the hand, from glTF files.
